No installation, account or payment is needed to play. Open the link in your phone’s usual browser and bookmark it to find it again. For reliable saving, use the same phone and browser each lesson rather than a Teams or ChatGPT preview.

You start with one wheat field and £15. Produce sells automatically, but every enterprise has running costs. You decide when it is sensible to grow.

  1. Read your balance and daily figures. Net profit is what remains after running costs.
  2. Open a purchase forecast. Compare price, extra profit, payback and cash left. Buying immediately is not always the best choice.
  3. Use the next-stage checklist. Develop different enterprises to unlock crops, animals, machinery and staff.
  4. Respond to random events. Read the situation, compare responses and explain your reason. Buffs help, debuffs create pressure, and counters protect against a problem.
  5. Finish with My report. Record what you learned and download the report to submit as your tutor asks.
Progress and written answers are stored on this browser only. On a shared computer, download your report before restarting for the next learner. Do not put personal or sensitive information in your answers.
Farm-business glossary and worked example
Gross income
Money from sales before running costs are taken away.
Running costs
Repeated spending such as feed, seed, wages, utilities and upkeep.
Net profit
Sales income minus running costs. A negative result is a loss.
Cash flow
Money moving into and out of the farm. A profitable farm can still run short of cash.
Unpaid bills
Costs the farm could not cover. Future cash pays them first, and new purchases wait until they are cleared.
Reserve
Cash kept available for a chosen number of days of costs.
Payback
How long extra net profit takes to recover the purchase price, if conditions stay the same.
Welfare
Meeting animals’ needs and providing suitable care. Essential care continues in every event option.
Buff, debuff and counter
A temporary benefit, a temporary disadvantage, or a response that fully or partly protects the farm from a problem.

Worked example: A purchase costs £100. It adds £8/day of sales and £3/day of costs. Extra net profit = £8 − £3 = £5/day. Payback = £100 ÷ £5 = 20 farm days. You still need enough cash for your other bills.

Tutor session suggestion · 50 minutes

0–5 min: Introduce income, costs and cash. 5–15 min: Compare two purchase forecasts. 15–35 min: Grow towards the next milestone and respond to events. 35–45 min: Discuss a decision with a partner. 45–50 min: Complete and download the learning report.

The figures are game assumptions, not current UK farm prices, a qualification assessment or a farm-business recommendation. Discuss the limits: simplified daily output, fixed capacity and short event durations. Random events mean students will face different situations.

One minute of active play equals one farm day. Your farm pauses for decisions, when this tab is hidden, and between sessions. Reports are not automatically sent to a tutor.
